Is It Safe to Take Benadryl for Sleep Every Night?

September 23, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: No. Diphenhydramine (Benadryl and most "PM" and "nighttime" sleep aids) is labeled for occasional sleeplessness, not nightly use. In a controlled trial, its sleepiness effect was gone by day 4 of regular dosing. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine suggests against it for chronic insomnia, and the American Geriatrics Society says adults 65 and older should avoid it.

What does the Drug Facts label actually say?

Store-brand sleep aids list diphenhydramine as the only active ingredient. A typical nighttime sleep aid label on DailyMed contains 50 mg per softgel. Its stated use is "relief of occasional sleeplessness." The word occasional matters. Nightly use for weeks is outside what the label describes.

The same label includes these warnings:

  • Stop use and ask a doctor if sleeplessness persists continuously for more than 2 weeks. The label adds that insomnia may be a symptom of a serious underlying medical illness.
  • Ask a doctor before use if you have a breathing problem such as emphysema or chronic bronchitis, glaucoma, or trouble urinating due to an enlarged prostate.
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist if you take sedatives or tranquilizers.
  • Avoid alcoholic drinks while using it.
  • Do not use in children under 12, or with any other product that contains diphenhydramine, even one used on the skin.

The 2-week line is not a formal dosing limit. It is a signal that the problem is no longer "occasional," and that a clinician should look for the cause. That last warning also matters in practice. Diphenhydramine is in many cold, allergy, and "PM" pain products, so doubling up by accident is easy.

Does Benadryl keep working if you take it every night?

Usually not for long. The best direct test is a randomized, double-blind crossover trial by Richardson and colleagues (2002). Fifteen healthy men took diphenhydramine 50 mg or placebo twice a day for 4 days. The team measured sleepiness objectively and by self-report, and tested psychomotor performance.

On day 1, diphenhydramine made people clearly sleepier and impaired their performance. By day 4, sleepiness on the drug could not be told apart from placebo, and the impairment was fully reversed. The authors concluded that tolerance was complete by the end of 3 days on that regimen.

Keep the limits in mind. The study was small, used daytime twice-daily dosing, and enrolled healthy young and middle-aged men, not people with insomnia. Still, the direction is clear. The drowsiness that helps on night one fades fast. The 2023 AGS Beers Criteria also notes that tolerance develops when first-generation antihistamines are used as hypnotics.

Why do you feel groggy the next day?

Diphenhydramine does not switch off at your alarm. MedlinePlus lists drowsiness, dizziness, muscle weakness, and dry mouth, nose, and throat as common side effects. It advises against driving or using machinery until you know how the drug affects you. Alcohol adds to the drowsiness.

Tolerance creates an odd mismatch. The sedation you wanted at bedtime fades, but side effects and slowed reactions can linger into the morning. The Beers panel adds that clearance falls with age, so older adults carry the drug longer. If you wake up foggy after a "PM" pill, the pill may be the cause.

What are anticholinergic effects, and who is most at risk?

Diphenhydramine blocks histamine, which causes drowsiness. It also blocks acetylcholine, a chemical messenger used for memory, attention, bladder control, gut movement, and saliva. That second action is the "anticholinergic" effect.

The 2023 American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria list oral diphenhydramine and doxylamine among the first-generation antihistamines older adults should avoid. The panel rates this recommendation as strong, based on moderate-quality evidence. Its rationale names:

  • High anticholinergic activity, with risk of confusion, dry mouth, constipation, and other toxicity.
  • Reduced drug clearance with advanced age.
  • Tolerance when used as a sleep aid.
  • Cumulative anticholinergic exposure linked to falls, delirium, and dementia, "even in younger adults."

The panel makes one exception. Diphenhydramine may be appropriate for acute treatment of severe allergic reactions. MedlinePlus gives the same message for people 65 and older. People with glaucoma, an enlarged prostate, or lung disease also need a doctor's input first, as the label says.

Does long-term use raise dementia risk?

The evidence shows an association, not proof of cause. The most cited study is Gray and colleagues in JAMA Internal Medicine (2015). It followed 3,434 adults aged 65 and older without dementia at the start. Over a mean of 7.3 years, 797 developed dementia.

The researchers used pharmacy records to add up 10 years of strong anticholinergic use. Compared with no use, the adjusted hazard ratio for dementia was 1.54 in the heaviest group. That group had more than 1,095 standardized daily doses, roughly daily use for more than 3 years. Lower exposure levels did not show a statistically clear increase.

Some honest limits:

  • It measured a drug class, not Benadryl alone. First-generation antihistamines were the second most common class, after tricyclic antidepressants. The paper does not give a separate result for diphenhydramine.
  • Over-the-counter use may be missed. The authors note that exposure could be misclassified because these antihistamines are sold without a prescription.
  • Pharmacy fills are not pills swallowed, and unmeasured confounding is possible, as in any observational study.

A much larger 2019 nested case-control study of 58,769 dementia patients in England found a similar pattern for total anticholinergic exposure. The adjusted odds ratio rose to 1.49 in the highest exposure group. The drug types it named with significant increases were antidepressants, antiparkinson drugs, antipsychotics, bladder antimuscarinics, and antiepileptics. That study used prescription records, so it could not capture most over-the-counter sleep aid use. The fair summary: heavy, years-long anticholinergic use is linked to dementia, and nightly Benadryl adds to that load.

What do sleep experts recommend instead?

The 2017 American Academy of Sleep Medicine guideline on drugs for chronic insomnia suggests that clinicians not use diphenhydramine for sleep onset or sleep maintenance insomnia. It is a weak recommendation under the GRADE system, which reflects limited evidence. The same guideline also suggests against melatonin and tryptophan for chronic insomnia.

For insomnia that lasts, first-line care is cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia. Our guide to CBT-I explains how it works. If a medicine is needed, a clinician can compare prescription options with better evidence. Our sleep medications comparison covers them. Poor sleep often travels with worry or low mood, and anxiety.md covers that overlap.

If you already take a sleep aid every night, talk with a clinician or pharmacist before changes. Stopping is usually simple, but a plan helps. Our guide to tapering off sleeping pills walks through the process.

The bottom line

An occasional Benadryl for a rough night is what the label allows for most healthy adults. Taking it every night is a different matter. The sleepiness fades within days, the morning fog and anticholinergic effects do not, and heavy long-term use is linked to dementia. Adults 65 and older should avoid it. If sleeplessness lasts more than 2 weeks, the label and sleep experts agree: see a clinician and treat the insomnia itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ZzzQuil or Unisom the same as Benadryl?

ZzzQuil and many store-brand nighttime sleep aids use diphenhydramine, the same active ingredient as Benadryl. Some Unisom products use diphenhydramine and others use doxylamine, a related antihistamine. Check the Drug Facts panel. The AGS Beers Criteria list both drugs as ones older adults should avoid.

How long can I take a diphenhydramine sleep aid?

The label is written for occasional sleeplessness. It tells you to stop and ask a doctor if sleeplessness persists continuously for more than 2 weeks. Tolerance to the sleepiness effect can appear within about 3 days of regular use.

Is Tylenol PM or Advil PM safer for nightly sleep?

Many PM pain relievers pair a pain reliever with diphenhydramine or doxylamine for the sleep effect. You get the same antihistamine concerns plus a daily dose of pain reliever you may not need. Do not combine them with other products that contain diphenhydramine.

Will stopping nightly Benadryl cause rebound insomnia?

There is little research on stopping diphenhydramine specifically, and a few restless nights are possible. Stopping is usually straightforward, but talk with a clinician or pharmacist about a plan. CBT-I techniques can help during the change.

Does the dementia link apply to younger adults?

Most studies enrolled people 55 or older, so the data for younger adults are thin. The 2023 Beers panel noted the link with falls, delirium, and dementia even in younger adults. Limiting total anticholinergic exposure over the years is a sensible goal at any age.
