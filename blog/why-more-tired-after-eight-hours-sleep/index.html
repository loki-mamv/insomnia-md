Why Do I Feel More Tired After Eight Hours of Sleep Than After Six?

The short answer: Eight hours in bed does not guarantee eight hours of good sleep. If you wake from deep sleep, you can feel groggy for 15 to 30 minutes or longer. If your alarm changes from day to day, your body clock drifts and mornings feel worse. And if breathing pauses or another condition break up your sleep, a long night can still leave you unrefreshed. Sleep quality and timing matter as much as the total.

Why do I feel groggy right after waking?

The heavy, slow feeling in the first minutes after waking has a name: sleep inertia. A 2019 review by Hilditch and McHill in Nature and Science of Sleep defines it as "the grogginess felt upon awakening" and notes it comes with real drops in thinking speed and accuracy (Hilditch and McHill, 2019). Most studies show performance returns to normal within 30 minutes of waking, sometimes within 15.

Two things make sleep inertia worse, according to that review. The first is prior sleep loss. The second is the time of day you wake, because the body clock has its own low points. A six-hour night that ends at your usual time can beat an eight-hour night that ends at an odd hour.

The review also flags a common belief: that waking from deep sleep is the main cause. It calls the evidence for this "mixed." Sleep depth plays a role, but sleep debt and timing matter at least as much.

Does it matter which sleep stage I wake from?

Sleep runs in cycles. NHLBI says each cycle "starts over every 80 to 100 minutes" and that "usually there are four to six cycles per night" (NHLBI, Stages of Sleep). The often-quoted 90-minute figure is an average, not a fixed number.

Stage 3 is deep sleep, also called slow-wave sleep. NINDS calls it "the period of deep sleep that you need to feel refreshed in the morning" and notes that "it occurs in longer periods during the first half of the night" (NINDS, Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep). NINDS also says it "may be difficult to awaken you" during this stage. REM periods get longer as the night goes on.

What this means in practice: your deep sleep is mostly done by the middle of the night. A later alarm usually lands in lighter sleep or REM, not deep sleep. If mornings still feel heavy, look at timing and sleep quality next.

Can sleeping longer make me more tired?

Sleeping in on days off shifts your internal clock. Wittmann and colleagues named this gap "social jetlag" in 2006. They described it as the discrepancy "between work and free days, between social and biological time" (Wittmann et al., 2006). In their sample of 501 volunteers, larger gaps went with worse sleep quality and wellbeing, most of all in late types.

An eight-hour night that starts and ends two hours later than usual is a small trip across time zones for your brain. NHLBI advises keeping the same schedule on weeknights and weekends and limiting the difference "to no more than about an hour" (NHLBI, Healthy Sleep Habits).

Long sleep is also linked to health problems, though the reason is not settled. A 2010 meta-analysis of 16 prospective studies and 1,382,999 participants found long sleep duration was associated with a relative risk of death of 1.30 (Cappuccio et al., 2010). That is an association, not proof of harm. Illness, depression, and poor sleep quality can all push people to sleep longer. Regularly needing more than nine hours and still feeling tired is worth mentioning to a doctor.

Could sleep apnea be breaking up my eight hours?

NHLBI defines sleep apnea as a condition "that occurs when your breathing stops and restarts many times while you sleep" (NHLBI, Sleep Apnea). Each pause can end with a brief arousal you never remember. The AASM explains that these "numerous arousals throughout the night" prevent "the high-quality sleep" your body needs, and lists "unrefreshing sleep" as a common symptom (AASM, Recognizing Sleep Apnea Warning Signs).

The AASM estimates nearly 30 million U.S. adults have obstructive sleep apnea, and about 23.5 million of those cases are undiagnosed. Signs to look for:

  • Loud snoring, especially with choking, gasping, or silent pauses noticed by a bed partner.
  • Morning headaches or a dry mouth on waking.
  • Falling asleep without meaning to during the day.
  • Trouble with focus, memory, or mood that does not match how long you slept.

Obesity, large tonsils, and hormone changes raise the risk, per NHLBI. If any of these sound familiar, read our guide to sleep apnea signs and ask about a sleep study.

What medical problems make sleep feel unrefreshing?

MedlinePlus lists several medical causes of ongoing tiredness beyond sleep disorders (MedlinePlus, Fatigue). The ones most often confused with "bad sleep" are:

  • Thyroid disease. NIDDK lists fatigue first among hypothyroidism symptoms, along with weight gain and trouble tolerating cold. It reports that nearly 5 out of 100 Americans ages 12 and older have hypothyroidism, and most cases are mild (NIDDK, Hypothyroidism). A TSH blood test is the usual first check. See thyroid.md for more.
  • Depression. NIMH lists "difficulty sleeping, waking too early in the morning, or oversleeping" and "fatigue, lack of energy, or feeling slowed down" among its symptoms (NIMH, Depression).
  • Anemia. NHLBI lists tiredness, weakness, shortness of breath, and paleness as symptoms, and notes mild anemia may cause no symptoms at all (NHLBI, Anemia Symptoms). A complete blood count finds it.
  • Medications. MedlinePlus names antidepressants, antihistamines, and medicines for nausea and pain as common causes of fatigue. Do not stop a medicine on your own. Ask the prescriber whether timing or dose can change.

MedlinePlus gives a clear threshold: "If you have been feeling tired for weeks, contact your health care provider."

What should I try first?

Start with schedule, not supplements. Both NHLBI and MedlinePlus give the same first instruction: "Go to bed and wake up at the same time every day" (MedlinePlus, Healthy Sleep). A fixed wake time, including weekends, anchors your body clock and shrinks social jet lag.

  1. Fix the wake time. Pick one time and hold it seven days a week. Keep any weekend shift under an hour, per NHLBI.
  2. Get morning light. MedlinePlus advises enough sunlight during the day. Light after waking sets the clock, though the sleep inertia review found light alone had limited effect on measured performance.
  3. Do not add time in bed to fix tiredness. The AASM's 2021 guideline strongly recommends CBT-I for chronic insomnia, and conditionally recommends sleep restriction therapy as a single treatment (AASM, 2021 guideline). The core idea is to match time in bed to time actually asleep. Our sleep restriction therapy guide explains the method.
  4. Keep naps short. NHLBI says adults should nap no more than 20 minutes. Longer naps reach deep sleep and bring their own grogginess.
  5. Watch caffeine timing. NHLBI notes caffeine effects can last up to 8 hours. A late-afternoon coffee can fragment the night that follows.

Give a consistent schedule two to three weeks. If you still wake unrefreshed, or if you wake in the middle of the night on a regular pattern, see our article on why you wake up at 3am and ask a clinician about a sleep study and basic blood work.

The bottom line

Feeling worse after eight hours than after six is common and usually explainable. The likely causes, in order: sleep inertia from a mistimed or delayed wake time, social jet lag from an irregular schedule, and fragmented sleep from undiagnosed sleep apnea. Medical causes such as thyroid disease, depression, anemia, and sedating medicines come next. Fix the wake time first. If weeks of a steady schedule do not help, get checked.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does morning grogginess normally last?

Most studies find thinking and reaction time return to normal within 30 minutes of waking, and sometimes within 15 minutes. A milder tail can last longer. Grogginess is worse after sleep loss and after waking at an unusual time of day.

Is it better to sleep in 90-minute multiples?

Not reliably. NHLBI says a cycle restarts every 80 to 100 minutes, and the length varies from person to person and night to night. A fixed wake time does more for morning alertness than counting cycles.

Why do I feel worse after sleeping in on the weekend?

A late wake time shifts your body clock, which researchers call social jet lag. Your brain treats a two-hour shift like a short trip across time zones. Keeping weekend wake time within about an hour of weekday wake time limits the effect.

How do I know if sleep apnea is the reason I wake up tired?

Loud snoring with gasping or breathing pauses, morning headaches, and dozing off during the day are the main warning signs. The AASM estimates about 23.5 million U.S. adults have undiagnosed obstructive sleep apnea. A sleep study confirms the diagnosis.

Which blood tests check for medical causes of tiredness?

A TSH test screens for hypothyroidism, and a complete blood count screens for anemia. Your clinician may also screen for depression and review your medications. MedlinePlus advises contacting a provider if tiredness has lasted for weeks.
