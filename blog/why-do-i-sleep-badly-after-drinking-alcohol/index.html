Why Do I Sleep Badly After Drinking Alcohol?

Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Alcohol is a sedative, so it helps you fall asleep faster. Then your body clears it, often within 4 to 5 hours, and sleep rebounds in the other direction: lighter, more broken, with more waking. Alcohol also delays and cuts REM sleep, relaxes the throat muscles that keep your airway open, and sends you to the bathroom. A 2025 meta-analysis of 27 studies found REM disruption after about two standard drinks.

Why does alcohol put you to sleep and then wake you up?

The night after drinking has two halves. A 2013 review in Alcoholism: Clinical and Experimental Research looked at all known studies in healthy volunteers. At all doses, alcohol shortened the time to fall asleep and made the first half of the night more consolidated. It also increased sleep disruption in the second half.

Sleep researchers call the second half a rebound. A review published by the National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism (NIAAA) explains it this way. Your body adjusts to alcohol during the first hours of sleep. When the alcohol is gone, those adjustments remain, and sleep variables swing past normal in the opposite direction. The result is more wake time and more light stage 1 sleep.

The timing fits how fast the body clears alcohol. The same review says breath alcohol falls by 0.01 to 0.02 percent per hour. A typical study level of 0.06 to 0.08 percent at bedtime is cleared within 4 to 5 hours of falling asleep. That is why so many people wake in the early hours after a night of drinking. Our article on why you wake up at 3am covers the other common causes.

What does alcohol do to REM and deep sleep?

The effect depends on the dose. NIAAA defines one U.S. standard drink as about 14 grams of pure alcohol. That is 12 ounces of regular beer, 5 ounces of wine, or 1.5 ounces of distilled spirits.

  • REM sleep. The 2025 meta-analysis in Sleep Medicine Reviews found that alcohol delayed the start of REM sleep and reduced its duration. The disruption appeared at a low dose (0.50 grams per kilogram of body weight or less, which the authors put at about two standard drinks). It became progressively worse as the dose went up.
  • Falling asleep. In the same analysis, only a high dose shortened the time to fall asleep. The authors put that dose at 0.85 grams per kilogram or more, about five standard drinks. They concluded that this likely makes the later REM disruption worse.
  • Deep sleep. The 2013 review found that most studies showed more slow wave (deep) sleep in the first half of the night. Deep sleep over the whole night increased at high doses.

More deep sleep early in the night does not mean better sleep overall. The cost comes later, in the second half. The 2025 analysis could not determine a clear effect on total sleep time, sleep efficiency, or time awake during the night. The REM findings are the most consistent result.

Does alcohol make snoring and sleep apnea worse?

Yes. Alcohol is a muscle relaxant, and that includes the muscles that hold the upper airway open. A meta-analysis of 14 randomized trials with 422 participants measured breathing during sleep with and without alcohol. The apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) counts breathing pauses per hour of sleep. After alcohol:

  • AHI rose by an average of 2.33 events per hour across all participants.
  • In people who snore, it rose by 4.20 events per hour.
  • In people with diagnosed obstructive sleep apnea, it rose by 7.10 events per hour.
  • Breathing pauses lasted longer, and the lowest blood oxygen level during the night fell further.

Long-term drinking habits matter too. A meta-analysis of 21 studies found that higher alcohol consumption was linked to a 25% higher risk of sleep apnea. The authors found evidence of publication bias, so the true figure may be smaller.

If a bed partner says you snore loudly or stop breathing after you drink, tell your doctor. Read our guide to the signs of sleep apnea first.

Why do you wake up to urinate or sweat after drinking?

Bathroom trips. MedlinePlus says the body normally makes less urine at night, which lets most people sleep 6 to 8 hours without urinating. It lists alcohol with or after dinner, and a lot of alcohol before bedtime, as common causes of waking to urinate.

Night sweats. The UK National Health Service lists alcohol use among the most common reasons for night sweats. In people who drink heavily every day, sweating can also be a withdrawal symptom. MedlinePlus lists sweating, a rapid heart rate, and insomnia among them.

How long before bed should you stop drinking?

No trial has set an exact cutoff. Two findings give a practical guide.

First, the Jackson Heart Sleep Study tracked 785 adults with wrist monitors for 5,164 nights. Alcohol within 4 hours of bedtime was linked to lower sleep efficiency that night. The drop was small, about 1 percentage point. It did not change total sleep time. Caffeine within 4 hours of bed showed no effect in that study, although other research supports a longer caffeine cutoff.

Second, the clearance rate matters. At 0.01 to 0.02 percent per hour, the NIAAA review estimates that a level of 0.06 to 0.08 percent takes 4 to 5 hours to clear. More drinks take longer.

Put together, the research supports two steps: drink less, and leave several hours between your last drink and bedtime. The more you drink, the longer the gap needs to be. In the 2025 meta-analysis, even about two drinks reduced REM sleep.

Why does alcohol backfire as a sleep aid?

The sedative effect does not last. The NIAAA review reports that tolerance to alcohol's sedative and sleep-stage effects develops within 3 nights.

A 2018 study in the journal Sleep tested this in people with insomnia and no history of alcohol problems. In the first experiment, 24 volunteers took alcohol or a placebo before bed. A dose of 0.6 grams per kilogram increased total sleep time and deep sleep on night 2. Those gains were lost by night 6. In a second experiment, volunteers who had six nights of bedtime alcohol later chose more alcohol refills than volunteers who had a placebo.

The link runs both ways. A second NIAAA review says sleep problems are more common in people with alcohol dependence, and that sleep problems may predispose some people to develop alcohol problems. Anxiety after a night of drinking can also make the next night harder. Our sibling site explains why anxiety is worse after drinking.

What happens to sleep when a heavy drinker stops?

Sleep usually gets worse before it gets better. MedlinePlus says withdrawal symptoms tend to start within 8 hours of the last drink and peak by 24 to 72 hours. They include insomnia, nightmares, sweating, and a rapid heart rate. Sleep changes may last for months.

Do not stop suddenly without medical advice if you drink heavily every day. Severe withdrawal, called delirium tremens, can cause seizures, hallucinations, and sudden severe confusion. MedlinePlus describes withdrawal as ranging from mild to life-threatening. A doctor can tell you if you need supervised treatment.

Sleep does improve with time. A 2015 review of insomnia in alcohol use disorder calls sobriety a necessary first-line treatment and says most patients will have some improvement. For insomnia that persists after 4 or more weeks of sobriety, it names brief behavioral therapies as the treatment of choice. They showed long-lasting benefit without making drinking outcomes worse.

The bottom line

Alcohol helps you fall asleep and then takes the sleep back. Your body clears it in the middle of the night, and the second half becomes light and broken. REM sleep drops after about two drinks. Snoring and sleep apnea get worse, and bathroom trips and sweating add more wake-ups. Drink less and leave several hours before bed. Do not use alcohol as a sleep aid: in the study that tested it, the benefit was gone by the sixth night. If you drink heavily every day, talk to a doctor before you stop.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does one drink affect sleep?

It may. A 2025 meta-analysis of 27 studies found that REM sleep was disrupted at low doses, which the authors put at about two standard drinks or less. The published summary does not report one drink separately. The effect grew with larger doses.

Is wine or beer better for sleep than liquor?

The research measures the dose of alcohol, not the type of drink. NIAAA says 12 ounces of regular beer, 5 ounces of wine, and 1.5 ounces of spirits each contain about 14 grams of pure alcohol. The same amount of alcohol counts the same, whatever the drink.

Why do I wake up early after drinking even when I am tired?

Your body clears alcohol at a steady rate, and the bedtime levels used in sleep studies are gone within 4 to 5 hours of falling asleep. Sleep then rebounds toward more waking and lighter sleep.

Can alcohol cause sleep apnea in someone who does not have it?

It can make breathing worse in anyone. In a meta-analysis of 14 randomized trials, alcohol raised the average number of breathing pauses per hour, with larger rises in snorers and people with sleep apnea. A separate meta-analysis linked higher alcohol consumption to a 25% higher risk of sleep apnea.

How long does it take for sleep to improve after quitting alcohol?

It varies. MedlinePlus says withdrawal symptoms peak by 24 to 72 hours and that sleep changes may last for months. A 2015 review says most people have some improvement with sobriety. See a doctor if insomnia continues after 4 or more weeks without alcohol.
