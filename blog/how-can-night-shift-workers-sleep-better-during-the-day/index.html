How Can Night Shift Workers Sleep Better During the Day?

Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Day sleep is hard because your body clock still runs on day time. Most night workers never fully adapt. Go to bed soon after the shift, in a very dark and quiet room, at a fixed time. Get bright light early in the shift, stop caffeine well before the shift ends, and nap before work. In a Cochrane review, melatonin added about 24 minutes of day sleep.

Why is day sleep shorter and lighter after a night shift?

Your body clock expects sleep at night. Night work asks for the opposite. Sleep specialists call this circadian misalignment.

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) addressed this in a draft clinical practice guideline on shift work disorder, released for public comment in 2025. It says most night workers do not shift their body clock to match night work. The result is shorter and less refreshing sleep.

The clock also sets a time limit on day sleep. A training course for nurses from NIOSH, the U.S. occupational health institute, explains it. If your clock has not adjusted, you have only a small window before its natural alerting signal starts. That signal tries to keep you awake. The course says the more sleep you get before 2 p.m., the better rested you will be. It adds that night workers usually get less sleep, and poorer sleep, than day workers.

What is shift work disorder?

Shift work disorder is a diagnosis. Hard sleep after a night shift is common, and it is not always a disorder. The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute says the disorder affects people who work at night or on a rotating schedule. It can cause insomnia, extreme tiredness, and sleepiness while working at night.

The AASM draft guideline says the disorder results when a work schedule routinely overlaps your usual sleep time for at least 3 months. It estimates that about 27% of adult shift workers have it (95% confidence interval, 21% to 33%).

Doctors usually ask for a sleep diary. The AASM's 2007 practice parameters say a sleep log is indicated when a circadian rhythm disorder is suspected. An overnight sleep study is not routine, but it can rule out another sleep disorder.

How should you set up your bedroom and sleep time?

The NIOSH course gives these steps for sleep after a night shift:

  • Go directly to bed when you get home. Do not use the early morning for chores or errands. That is your prime sleep time.
  • Make the room very dark. Use blackout shades or drapes on bedroom and bathroom windows. Block the light under doors. If you get up, use a dim red nightlight.
  • Cut noise and interruptions. Use earplugs or white noise. Stop phone interruptions. Ask family and friends not to disturb you.
  • Sleep as long as possible.

A fixed sleep time matters too. The studies behind the AASM draft used a dark room and a sleep mask. Sleep started within 90 to 120 minutes of the end of the shift and lasted 7 to 8 hours.

If day sleep stays poor, ask about cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I). The AASM draft suggests it for shift work disorder with day sleep problems. The evidence is very low certainty.

When should you get light and when should you avoid it?

Light sets the body clock and makes you more alert. The NIOSH course advises two steps for people on full-time nights:

  • Increase your light exposure in the first half of the shift. You do not need a light box. Spend as much time as you can in a brightly lit area.
  • Reduce your light exposure in the second half of the shift, so that sleep comes more easily at home.

Sunglasses on the way home can limit morning light. They also carry a risk. NIOSH warns that if you are very sleepy, sunglasses block the alerting effect of light and can make drowsy driving worse. Its safest advice: wear them after a night shift only if someone else drives.

The evidence is weak. A 2016 Cochrane review of 17 trials looked at bright light, naps, and other non-drug steps. It called the results inconclusive. The AASM draft still suggests bright light during the night shift. It adds that a full clock shift is not a realistic goal for people on rotating shifts.

Do naps and caffeine help or hurt day sleep?

Naps. A nap before a night shift helps alertness. A NIOSH module on napping cites a study of nurses and nursing aides. Those who took a 1.5-hour nap from 3:30 to 5:00 p.m. reported more alertness in the second half of their first night shift. The AASM draft suggests a nap before the night shift. It warns about sleep inertia, the grogginess after you wake. Allow time for it to clear before you drive. Our article on whether napping ruins night sleep covers naps for people on a day schedule.

Caffeine. Caffeine helps on the job. A Cochrane review of 13 trials found that it reduced errors and improved attention compared with placebo. The problem is timing. NIOSH puts the half-life of caffeine at 5 to 6 hours, and much longer in some people. It advises night workers to have caffeine at the start of the shift and stop several hours before the shift ends. The AASM draft says caffeine within six hours of sleep reduces sleep length and quality. See our guide on when to stop caffeine before bed.

Does melatonin help you sleep during the day?

A little. The 2014 Cochrane review pooled seven trials with 263 participants. Melatonin taken after the night shift added about 24 minutes of day sleep compared with placebo. The trials used 1 to 10 milligrams, and the review found no dose-response effect. Melatonin did not shorten the time to fall asleep. The evidence was low quality.

The AASM draft suggests melatonin for day sleep problems after the night shift, with very low certainty. It gives two cautions. Melatonin can make you sleepy, so do not drive or work until that passes. And supplement quality varies: one study found actual melatonin content from 83% below to 478% above the label. Our article on the truth about melatonin has more.

The same draft suggests against triazolam and other benzodiazepines for day sleep in shift work disorder.

Which prescription drugs are approved for shift work disorder?

Two. The FDA labels for modafinil and armodafinil (Nuvigil) include shift work disorder. Both are indicated to improve wakefulness in adults with excessive sleepiness from the disorder. The labels say to take the drug about 1 hour before the start of the work shift.

These drugs keep you awake at work. They do not help you sleep in the day. The armodafinil label warns that your level of wakefulness may not return to normal. It lists insomnia among the most common side effects, with headache, nausea, and dizziness. Both drugs are Schedule IV controlled substances. Serious rashes that required a hospital stay have been reported.

The benefit is modest. In the Cochrane review, armodafinil reduced sleepiness by about one point on a 10-point scale in people with shift work disorder. There were no trials in shift workers without the diagnosis.

Mood matters too. The AASM draft lists mood disorders among the health risks linked to shift work. If low mood lasts, our sibling site depression.md explains the signs and treatments.

The bottom line

You sleep worse in the day because your body clock stays on day time. Go to bed soon after the shift and get as much sleep as you can before 2 p.m. Make the room very dark and quiet. Get bright light early in the shift and less light later. Have caffeine early and stop several hours before the shift ends. Nap before work. Melatonin may add about 24 minutes. If sleepiness or poor sleep continues for 3 months, ask a doctor about shift work disorder.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hours of sleep do night shift workers need?

In the studies behind the AASM draft guideline, the fixed day sleep period lasted 7 to 8 hours. NIOSH advises night workers to sleep as long as possible, because they usually get less sleep than day workers.

Should I go to sleep right after a night shift or stay up for a while?

Go to bed soon. NIOSH advises night workers to go directly to bed when they get home and says sleep before 2 p.m. is the most restful. If your body clock has not adjusted, its alerting signal starts later in the day and makes sleep harder.

Is it safe to wear sunglasses on the drive home after a night shift?

Only if someone else drives. NIOSH warns that sunglasses block the alerting effect of light, which can make drowsy driving worse when you are very sleepy. The AASM draft guideline gives the same caution about dark glasses and driving.

Does the body ever get used to working nights?

Usually not fully. The AASM draft guideline says the body clocks of most shift workers do not adapt to the work schedule. Most people also return to night sleep on days off. The draft calls partial alignment a reasonable goal for people on steady nights.

Is shift work disorder the same as insomnia?

No. Shift work disorder is a circadian rhythm disorder caused by a work schedule that overlaps your usual sleep time. It can cause insomnia, and it can also cause strong sleepiness during the shift. The AASM draft guideline says the pattern must last at least 3 months.
