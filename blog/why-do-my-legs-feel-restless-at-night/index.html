Why Do My Legs Feel Restless When I Try to Fall Asleep?

The short answer: An urge to move your legs that starts when you lie down, eases when you move, and is worse in the evening is the pattern of restless legs syndrome (RLS). In a survey of 15,391 adults, 7.2% reported RLS symptoms in the past year. Low iron, pregnancy, kidney failure, and some common medicines can cause it or make it worse. It is treatable, and the first test is usually a blood test for iron.

What is restless legs syndrome, and how common is it?

The National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS) describes RLS as a neurological condition that causes an irresistible urge to move the legs. It is also called Willis-Ekbom disease.

The urge usually comes with an unpleasant feeling in the legs. NINDS lists aching, throbbing, pulling, itching, crawling, and creeping. MedlinePlus adds tingling and burning.

The timing is what hurts sleep. Symptoms build in the late afternoon and evening and peak when you are still. NINDS notes that walking gives relief, but the sensations often return as soon as you stop. That makes it hard to fall asleep and hard to get back to sleep after waking.

It is common. In the REST general population study, 5.0% of adults had symptoms at least weekly. A smaller group, 2.7%, had symptoms at least twice a week that were moderately or severely distressing. Of that group, 81% had discussed their symptoms with a primary care doctor, and only 6.2% of those were given a diagnosis of RLS. NINDS says RLS most often begins in middle age, is more common in women, and often runs in families.

How do doctors diagnose restless legs syndrome?

There is no scan or lab test that confirms RLS. NINDS says a clinician makes the diagnosis from your symptoms, medical history, medicines, family history, and a physical and neurological exam. The questions follow the consensus criteria of the International Restless Legs Syndrome Study Group, which were updated in 2014 to five essential criteria:

  1. An urge to move the legs, usually with uncomfortable sensations.
  2. The urge starts or gets worse during rest, such as lying down or sitting.
  3. Movement, such as walking or stretching, eases it for as long as you keep moving.
  4. It is worse in the evening or at night than during the day.
  5. Another condition does not better explain the symptoms.

Blood tests do not confirm RLS, but they look for causes. NINDS lists low iron and kidney failure among the things a clinician checks.

What causes or triggers restless legs?

In most cases the cause is unknown. NINDS points to two leads: low levels of iron in the brain, and a problem in the basal ganglia, the brain region that uses dopamine to control movement. Several conditions and medicines are linked to RLS:

  • Low iron. The MedlinePlus medical encyclopedia lists iron deficiency and anemia among linked conditions.
  • Pregnancy. A 2026 meta-analysis of 35 studies and 56,989 people put the pooled prevalence in pregnant women at 21.9%. NINDS says symptoms are most likely in the last trimester and usually disappear after delivery.
  • Kidney failure. A 2024 meta-analysis found a pooled prevalence of 27.2% in people on hemodialysis.
  • Other conditions. NINDS and MedlinePlus list nerve damage (neuropathy), diabetes, Parkinson's disease, and sleep apnea.
  • Medicines. NINDS names some anti-nausea drugs, antipsychotic drugs, antidepressants that increase serotonin, and some antihistamines. Diphenhydramine, the antihistamine in many over-the-counter sleep aids, is covered in our post on taking Benadryl for sleep every night.
  • Alcohol, nicotine, and caffeine. MedlinePlus says all three may make symptoms worse.

Do not stop a prescribed medicine because of this list. An antidepressant or antipsychotic that worsens RLS may still be the right medicine for you. Ask the prescriber if a change is possible.

Is it restless legs, a sleep start, or a leg cramp?

  • Restless legs. You are awake. You feel an urge to move, and you choose to move to get relief. It returns each time you lie still.
  • Sleep start (hypnic jerk). A single sudden jolt as you drift off, with no urge beforehand. It is over in an instant. See our post on why you jerk awake when falling asleep.
  • Leg cramp. MedlinePlus defines a muscle cramp as a sudden, involuntary contraction that can be painful and lasts a few seconds to several minutes. Stretching or gently massaging the muscle helps. A cramp is a tight, hard muscle. RLS is an urge to move.

A fourth pattern is periodic limb movement of sleep (PLMS). NINDS describes involuntary leg twitches every 15 to 40 seconds during sleep. Most people with RLS also have PLMS, but most people with PLMS do not have RLS.

How is restless legs syndrome treated?

The current standard is the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) clinical practice guideline, published in January 2025. It updates the AASM guidance from 2012.

Iron tests come first. The guideline says clinicians should regularly test iron studies, including ferritin and transferrin saturation, in all patients with clinically significant RLS. It cites consensus thresholds for adults: consider iron if ferritin is 75 ng/mL or lower, or transferrin saturation is below 20%. The guideline states that these thresholds differ from those used for the general population and have not been empirically tested. Iron is a decision for your clinician, because too much iron is harmful. Do not start it on your own.

Then remove what makes it worse. The guideline names alcohol, caffeine, antihistamine, serotonergic and antidopaminergic medicines, and untreated obstructive sleep apnea. Our post on the signs of sleep apnea lists what to watch for.

Medicine classes. The AASM makes strong recommendations for three related medicines: gabapentin enacarbil, gabapentin, and pregabalin. It also makes a strong recommendation for intravenous ferric carboxymaltose in people with the right iron status, and a conditional one for oral ferrous sulfate.

Dopamine agonists are no longer standard. The guideline suggests against the standard use of pramipexole, ropinirole, the rotigotine patch, and levodopa. The reason is augmentation, a long-term adverse effect of these medicines. NINDS puts it plainly: dopaminergic drugs can help, but long-term use may worsen symptoms. The AASM says they may still suit people who value short-term relief more than the long-term risk. If you take one now, do not stop it suddenly. Talk to your prescriber.

The guideline also gives conditional recommendations for opioids such as extended-release oxycodone and for bilateral high-frequency peroneal nerve stimulation. NINDS lists self-care that may help: moderate regular exercise, a regular sleep schedule, leg massage, a warm bath, and a heating pad or ice pack.

When should you see a doctor?

MedlinePlus advises contacting a provider if you have symptoms of RLS, if your sleep is disrupted, or if symptoms get worse. Bring a list of every medicine and supplement you take, including over-the-counter sleep aids. Ask for your ferritin number.

RLS is also linked to mood. In one meta-analysis of 24 studies, about 30% of people with RLS had depression or a depressive state. Mention low mood if you have it. depression.md covers depression and its treatment.

The bottom line

Restless legs at bedtime is a recognized neurological condition with clear criteria: an urge to move, brought on by rest, eased by movement, and worse at night. About 7% of adults report symptoms in a year. The 2025 AASM guideline starts with iron testing and a review of medicines, and it prefers gabapentin-type medicines to dopamine agonists. If this pattern keeps you awake, describe it to a clinician in those four terms and ask for iron studies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can low iron cause restless legs even if I am not anemic?

Yes, it can. The 2025 American Academy of Sleep Medicine guideline asks clinicians to check ferritin and transferrin saturation in everyone with clinically significant restless legs syndrome. It cites a ferritin threshold of 75 ng/mL for adults, which differs from the thresholds used for the general population. Ask your clinician before you take iron, because too much iron is harmful.

Does Benadryl make restless legs worse?

It can. NINDS lists some antihistamines among the medicines that may aggravate restless legs symptoms, and the sleep medicine guideline names antihistamine medicines as a factor to address first. Diphenhydramine, the active ingredient in Benadryl, is an antihistamine. If your legs feel worse on nights you take it, tell your clinician or pharmacist.

Do restless legs in pregnancy go away?

In most cases, yes. NINDS says restless legs syndrome in pregnancy is most likely in the last trimester and usually disappears after delivery. A 2026 meta-analysis put the pooled prevalence in pregnant women at 21.9%. Tell your prenatal clinician, because treatment choices in pregnancy need a safety review.

What is augmentation in restless legs syndrome?

Augmentation is a long-term adverse effect of dopamine medicines such as pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine, and levodopa. NINDS notes that long-term use of dopaminergic drugs may worsen symptoms. It is the reason the 2025 sleep medicine guideline suggests against the standard use of these medicines. Do not stop one suddenly. Talk to your prescriber.

Is restless legs syndrome the same as periodic limb movements?

No. Restless legs syndrome is an urge to move that you feel while awake. Periodic limb movements are involuntary twitches during sleep, which NINDS says occur every 15 to 40 seconds. Most people with restless legs syndrome also have periodic limb movements, but most people with periodic limb movements do not have restless legs syndrome.
